Why This Work Matters

Traditional foods are at the center of culture, community, and wellbeing across the region — but access has changed.

Across the Aleutian Pribilof Islands, fish and seafood, seals and sea lions, and berries have always been central to the culture and wellbeing of Unangan (Aleut) communities. But over the past decades, access to these traditional foods has decreased for many, while the remoteness of the region makes store-bought food expensive, difficult to access, and often quick to spoil.

This project is working in partnership with Regional Food Sovereignty Council members to understand how strengthening food sovereignty — a community's right to define and govern its own food system — can improve health and wellbeing region-wide. The work builds on a partnership between community members, the research team, and regional organizations dating back to 2019.

Our Approach: Three Aims

A step-by-step process, co-led with the region across three years.

Aim 1

Build a Shared Framework

Using concept mapping with the Regional Food Sovereignty Council, this aim co-creates a Food Sovereignty and Wellbeing Conceptual Framework grounded in community knowledge and experience.

Aim 2

Build a Regional Toolkit for Programs & Research

Drawing on a review of existing resources and community priorities, here we aim to identify culturally and locally relevant ways to improve food sovereignty and health through programs and research. These elements may also be used to measure success and different outcomes, depending on how they are incorporated into programs and/or reasearch. shared indicators and measures — from traditional food access to clinical biomarkers — for a regional research toolkit.

Aim 3

Pilot a Community Intervention

In partnership with the Aleut Commuinty of St. Paul Island, we will pilot a food sovereignty and wellbeing program, likely centered on the themes of traditional food harvesting, wellbeing, and storytelling.

Join the Regional Food Sovereignty Council

The RFSC brings together community members and leaders, Elders, youth, and community leaders from the Tribes and communities in the region to guide this work — from shaping the conceptual framework to piloting the St. Paul program.
